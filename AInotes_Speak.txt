Sample 1: 
Symptomatic Irreversible Pulpitis & Symptomatic Apical Periodontitis (Endodontics)
Transcription: "Patient Sarah Jenkins, 34, presents with constant, throbbing pain in her lower right jaw for 3 days. She states the pain lingers for hours and wakes her up at night. On examination of tooth 46, there is a deep disto-occlusal composite restoration with secondary caries. Percussion is highly positive (+), palpation is negative, and mobility is normal (Grade 0). Cold test triggers a sharp, lingering pain lasting over 45 seconds. Periapical X-ray shows deep disto-occlusal radiolucency approaching the pulp chamber, with a slightly widened mesial apical PDL space. No swelling is present. My assessment is symptomatic irreversible pulpitis with symptomatic apical periodontitis of tooth 46. Today, I performed a pulpectomy. I accessed the pulp chamber under local anesthesia (Articaine 4% with 1:100k epi), extirpated the pulpal tissue, irrigated with 2.5% NaOCl, placed Calcium Hydroxide as an intracanal medicament, and sealed with Cavit temporary filling. I advised avoiding chewing on the right side and returning in 2 weeks for root canal completion. Prescribed Ibuprofen 600mg every 6 hours as needed for post-op discomfort."
Sample 2: 
Pulp Necrosis & Chronic Apical Abscess (Root Canal Therapy)
Transcription: "Patient Michael Chang, 45, is here for the evaluation of a dark upper front tooth (tooth 11). He reports no active pain but noticed a small, painless bump on his upper gums. He has a history of trauma to the tooth 15 years ago. On examination, tooth 11 is visibly discolored (grayish). There is a patent sinus tract on the buccal mucosa near the apex of tooth 11. Cold and electric pulp tests (EPT) are non-responsive. Percussion is mildly positive. I traced the sinus tract with a gutta-percha cone and took a periapical X-ray, which reveals a large, well-defined 6mm radiolucency at the apex of tooth 11. My assessment is pulp necrosis with a chronic apical abscess on tooth 11. Today, I initiated root canal therapy. I prepared and shaped the canal to size 40/.04, disinfected it thoroughly with sodium hypochlorite, and obturated using gutta-percha and AH-plus sealer with warm vertical condensation. I placed a glass-ionomer base and restored the access cavity permanently with composite resin. No antibiotics are indicated because the infection has an active pathway to drain (sinus tract). Advised patient to monitor for any sudden swelling."
Sample 3: 
Cracked Tooth Syndrome (Restorative & Prosthodontics)
Transcription: "Patient Fatima Al-Farsi, 29, presents complaining of sharp pain when chewing hard foods on the lower left side for 2 weeks. The pain is brief and stops immediately upon releasing bite pressure. On examination, tooth 36 has a large mesio-occluso-distal (MOD) amalgam filling. Transillumination reveals a distinct mesial marginal ridge crack extending into the central groove. Bite test using a Tooth-Slooth is highly positive on the disto-lingual cusp. Cold test is positive with a normal, non-lingering response. Periapical radiograph shows a deep amalgam restoration but no periapical pathology; PDL space is normal. My assessment is cracked tooth syndrome of tooth 36 with a healthy, vital pulp. Today, I removed the old amalgam filling under local anesthesia, verified that the crack did not extend to the pulpal floor, prepared the tooth for a full-coverage monolithic zirconia crown, and fabricated an acrylic temporary crown cemented with Temp-Bond to splint the crack. I advised avoiding hard foods on the left side and returning in 10 days for crown cementation."
Sample 4:
 Stage I, Grade A Periodontitis (Periodontal/Hygiene)
Transcription: "Patient Liam O'Connor, 42, is here for a routine cleaning. He reports generalized bleeding gums during daily brushing. On clinical examination, there is generalized moderate plaque and calculus accumulation, particularly on the lingual surfaces of the lower anterior teeth (31-41). Periodontal charting reveals pocket depths of 3-4mm generalized, with localized 5mm pockets on the mesial of tooth 16 and 26. Bleeding on probing (BOP) is present at 35% of sites. There is 1-2mm of recession on the buccal of tooth 14 and 24. No mobility detected. Bitewing radiographs show localized mild horizontal bone loss (less than 15% of root length) at the interproximal spaces of molar regions. My assessment is Stage I, Grade A generalized periodontitis. Today, I performed full-mouth scaling and root planing (SRP) under topical anesthetic, followed by polishing and a 5% sodium fluoride varnish application. I provided thorough oral hygiene instructions, demonstrating the modified Bass brushing technique and emphasizing the daily use of interdental brushes. Scheduled a 4-week periodontal re-evaluation."
Sample 5:
 Peri-Implantitis (Implant & Surgical Care)
Transcription: "Patient Robert Taylor, 58, presents with a dull ache and a bad taste coming from his upper right implant (position tooth 14), which was placed 5 years ago. On examination, the implant-supported crown at position 14 is secure. The peri-implant mucosa is erythematous, edematous, and bleeds profusely upon gentle probing. There is purulent exudate upon digital palpation of the buccal tissue. Probing depths around the implant range from 6 to 7mm. The implant is stable with no signs of mobility. A periapical X-ray shows saucer-shaped crestal bone loss of approximately 3.5mm around the implant threads, indicating loss of bone support beyond physiological remodeling. My assessment is peri-implantitis at position 14. Today, I performed non-surgical debridement of the implant threads using plastic curettes and titanium-tipped scalers, followed by irrigation of the pocket with 0.12% Chlorhexidine gluconate. I placed local minocycline microspheres (Arestin) into the deepest pockets. Prescribed Chlorhexidine mouthrinse twice daily for 2 weeks. Scheduled a re-evaluation in 4 weeks to determine if surgical pocket reduction or bone grafting is required."
Sample 6:
 Acute Pericoronitis (Oral Surgery/Third Molar)
Transcription: "Patient Priya Patel, 22, presents with severe pain, mild facial swelling, and limited mouth opening (trismus) on the lower right side for 4 days. On examination, tooth 48 is partially erupted and mesioangularly impacted. A highly inflamed, swollen operculum covers the distal-occlusal surface of the tooth. Purulent discharge is visible upon gentle pressure under the tissue flap. The patient's maximum interincisal opening is limited to 24mm due to muscle spasm. There is palpable, tender right submandibular lymphadenopathy, but no airway compromise. Panoramic X-ray confirms tooth 48 is mesially impacted against the distal of 47. My assessment is acute pericoronitis of tooth 48. Today, I gently debrided and flushed the subopercular space with sterile saline and 0.2% Chlorhexidine. Due to the presence of systemic signs (trismus and lymphadenopathy), antibiotic therapy is indicated. Prescribed Amoxicillin 500mg, taken 3 times daily for 7 days, along with Ibuprofen 600mg every 6 hours for pain. I advised warm saline rinses 4 times daily and scheduled a follow-up in 1 week to plan the surgical extraction of tooth 48 once the acute phase resolves."
Sample 7:
 Deep Dentinal Caries & Direct Pulp Cap (Conservative Operative)
Transcription: "Patient David Miller, 31, is here because food catches in his lower left molar (tooth 37). He reports mild, transient sensitivity to sweet foods that resolves immediately. On examination, tooth 37 has a deep occlusal-buccal carious lesion. Cold test is positive with a normal, non-lingering response. Percussion and palpation are negative. Periapical radiograph shows a deep radiolucency on the occlusal of tooth 37 closely approaching the pulp chamber, but with an intact lamina dura and no periapical lucency. My assessment is deep dentinal caries with a healthy, vital pulp (reversible pulpitis) of tooth 37. Today, under local infiltration, I executed caries removal. During final excavation of the deepest decay, a pinpoint (<0.5mm) mechanical pulpal exposure occurred. The exposure site exhibited a clean, light red bleed which was easily controlled with a sterile saline-soaked cotton pellet within 1 minute. I performed a direct pulp cap using Mineral Trioxide Aggregate (MTA), applied a resin-modified glass ionomer (RMG_I) liner, and restored the tooth permanently with a bonded composite resin restoration. Advised the patient that mild sensitivity is normal but to return if they experience spontaneous, throbbing pain."
Sample 8:
 Alveolar Osteitis / Dry Socket (Post-Operative Complication)
Transcription: "Patient James Wilson, 27, returns to the clinic 4 days post-extraction of tooth 38 complaining of an intense, throbbing pain that started yesterday. He states the pain radiates to his ear and jaw and is not relieved by over-the-counter ibuprofen. On examination of the tooth 38 extraction site, the socket is empty of a healthy blood clot, exposing bare, sensitive alveolar bone. There is a strong, foul odor (halitosis) from the area, but no localized swelling, no purulence, and no systemic fever. A post-op radiograph was taken, confirming no retained root tips or foreign debris in the socket. My assessment is alveolar osteitis (dry socket) of the tooth 38 socket. Today, I gently irrigated the extraction socket with warm sterile saline to remove food debris. I then placed a palliative dry socket dressing containing eugenol (Alveogyl) loosely into the socket to coat the exposed bone. The patient reported immediate relief within 10 minutes. I advised him to avoid using drinking straws, smoking, or vigorous rinsing. Scheduled a follow-up in 48 hours for dressing evaluation."
Sample 9:
 Acute Apical Abscess & Emergency Incision & Drainage (Surgical/Endodontic)
Transcription: "Patient Elena Rostova, 39, presents with severe, localized throbbing pain and significant swelling on her upper left cheek for 2 days. On examination, there is a visible fluctuant swelling in the buccal vestibule adjacent to tooth 24. Tooth 24 has a fractured coronal restoration with gross decay. The tooth is extremely sensitive to touch and percussion (++). Cold test is non-responsive. The patient is afebrile, but has tender left submandibular lymph nodes. Periapical radiograph shows a diffuse, ill-defined radiolucency around the apex of tooth 24. My assessment is pulp necrosis with an acute apical abscess of tooth 24. Today, I initiated emergency treatment. Under local anesthesia, I performed an intraoral incision and drainage (I&D) of the vestibular swelling, releasing a large volume of purulent exudate. I placed a small sterile rubber drain to maintain patency. I then accessed the root canal of tooth 24, extirpated necrotic pulp tissue, instrumented, and left the canal open for 24 hours to assist drainage. I prescribed Amoxicillin 500mg with Clavulanic Acid (Augmentin) 625mg twice daily for 5 days due to spreading facial swelling, and scheduled the patient to return tomorrow to remove the drain and close the access cavity."
Sample 10:
 Minor Recurrent Aphthous Ulcer (Oral Pathology)
Transcription: "Patient Emily Watson, 21, presents complaining of a highly painful sore on the inside of her lower lip for 3 days, making it difficult to eat, drink, or speak. On intraoral clinical examination, there is a single, well-demarcated circular ulcer approximately 4mm in diameter on the labial mucosa of the lower lip. The lesion has a yellow-white necrotic center (pseudomembrane) surrounded by a distinct, raised erythematous halo. The base of the ulcer is soft, and there is no induration of the margins and no associated lymphadenopathy. The patient has a history of similar self-limiting ulcers during university exam periods. My assessment is a minor recurrent aphthous ulcer of the lower labial mucosa. Today, I applied a topical anesthetic gel (Benzocaine 20%) in-office to provide temporary pain relief. I educated the patient on avoiding triggers such as sodium lauryl sulfate (SLS) toothpastes and sharp foods. Prescribed Triamcinolone acetonide 0.1% dental paste (Orabase) to be applied to the lesion 3 times daily after meals to accelerate healing. Advised the patient that the ulcer should resolve spontaneously within 7 to 10 days."
